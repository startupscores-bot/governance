# CAIPE Project Charter

## 1. Identity

- **Name:** Community AI Platform Engineering
- **Short name:** CAIPE (pronounced "cape")
- **Home:** [caipe.io](https://caipe.io)
- **GitHub organization:** [caipe-io](https://github.com/caipe-io)

## 2. Mission

CAIPE is an open source platform for building, operating, and governing
multi-agent systems for platform engineering and cloud native operations.

The project provides a vendor-neutral place for the community to develop:

- interoperable agents and agentic workflows;
- secure integrations with operational tools and platforms;
- reusable knowledge, skills, and reference implementations;
- deployment patterns for development and production environments; and
- transparent practices for safety, reliability, and project governance.

## 3. Principles

CAIPE is governed according to these principles:

- **Open:** Discussions, decisions, roadmaps, and meeting records are public by
  default.
- **Community-led:** Authority is earned through sustained contribution and
  exercised for the project, not an employer.
- **Vendor-neutral:** No organization controls the project through committee
  concentration, trademarks, infrastructure, or private decision making.
- **Interoperable:** Project work favors open protocols, portable interfaces,
  and integrations that avoid lock-in.
- **Secure and responsible:** Security, privacy, identity, and human oversight
  are design requirements.
- **Inclusive:** Participation is open to anyone who follows the
  [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
- **Merit-based:** Responsibility follows demonstrated contribution, sound
  judgment, and constructive community participation.

## 4. Scope

The project scope includes software, documentation, examples, deployment
artifacts, community programs, and other assets accepted into the `caipe-io`
organization by the Steering Committee.

Each project repository must identify its maintainers, contribution process,
release process, security policy, and decision-making boundaries.

## 5. Roles

### Contributor

Anyone who contributes code, documentation, design, testing, issue triage,
community support, or other project work.

### Maintainer

A contributor trusted to review and merge changes within a defined repository
or component. Maintainers are recorded in that project's ownership files and
are responsible for project health, releases, and contributor development.

Maintainer selection and removal happen in the relevant repository under its
documented process. If no process exists, the Steering Committee approves the
change after a public nomination and review period.

### Working Group Lead

A contributor responsible for the operation and transparent reporting of an
approved working group. Leads do not gain authority beyond the scope delegated
in the group's charter.

### Steering Committee Member

A contributor elected or seated under
[STEERING-COMMITTEE.md](STEERING-COMMITTEE.md). Members owe their duty to the
project as a whole and must disclose conflicts of interest.

## 6. Steering Committee

The CAIPE Steering Committee is the project's highest decision-making body.

It is responsible for:

- project strategy and long-term direction;
- admission, archival, and transfer of project repositories;
- delegation to maintainers and working groups;
- organization-wide policies and GitHub administration;
- trademark, domain, and shared infrastructure stewardship;
- budget and foundation relationships, if any;
- escalated technical and governance disputes;
- Code of Conduct enforcement oversight; and
- succession and healthy distribution of project leadership.

The committee does not replace repository maintainers in routine technical
decisions. Its composition, elections, and accountability rules are defined in
[STEERING-COMMITTEE.md](STEERING-COMMITTEE.md).

## 7. Decisions

The project seeks consensus first. Decisions must be traceable to a public
issue, pull request, or meeting record except for confidential conduct,
security, legal, or personnel matters.

Voting thresholds, review windows, recusals, and appeal procedures are defined
in [DECISION-MAKING.md](DECISION-MAKING.md).

## 8. Working Groups

The Steering Committee may create time-bounded or persistent working groups.
All working groups are open to community participation and operate under a
public charter. See [working-groups/README.md](working-groups/README.md).

## 9. Assets and neutrality

Project source, governance records, domains, package namespaces, release
credentials, and shared infrastructure must be controlled by the project or a
neutral fiscal or legal host. Access must not depend on a single employer.

At least two unaffiliated administrators should be able to recover each
critical project asset where the service permits it.

## 10. Amendments

Charter changes require:

1. a public proposal;
2. at least 14 calendar days for community review; and
3. approval by two-thirds of all seated Steering Committee members.

Amendments take effect when the approved pull request is merged.
